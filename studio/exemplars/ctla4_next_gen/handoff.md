## Big picture

CTLA-4 was the first checkpoint approved (Yervoy, 2011), but toxicity kept it a sidekick to PD-1 drugs. Next-gen drugs aim to delete regulatory T cells (Tregs) inside tumors and spare healthy tissue. The first next-gen antibody with randomized survival data, gotistobart, nearly doubled median survival vs chemo.

**How the next-gen designs work**

| Approach | How it works | Assets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fc-enhanced depletion | A boosted Fc recruits NK cells and macrophages to kill CTLA-4-high Tregs in the tumor | Botensilimab (Agenus), porustobart (Solstice / Harbour BioMed), vilastobart (Xilio) |
| pH-sensitive recycling | Releases CTLA-4 inside the cell so it recycles instead of being degraded, sparing Tregs in healthy tissue | Gotistobart (BioNTech / OncoC4) |
| Masked, tumor-activated | Stays inactive until tumor proteases (MMPs) cut the mask off | Vilastobart (Xilio) |
| Short half-life | About 4–5 days, so side effects shouldn't linger | Porustobart |
| PD-1 + CTLA-4 in one product | Bispecifics, a co-formulation, and a trispecific that adds VEGF | Cadonilimab (Akeso), volrustomig (AstraZeneca), lorigerlimab (MacroGenics), quavonlimab + pembrolizumab (Merck), CS2009 (CStone) |
| Non-blocking depletion | Binds CTLA-4 without blocking its ligands, then depletes tumor Tregs | GIGA-564 (GigaGen) |
| Toxin payload | An engineered toxin body kills CTLA-4+ Tregs directly, with no effector cells needed | MT-8421 (Molecular Templates) |

A 2026 Annual Review of Medicine paper reached the same view: Fc-optimized anti-CTLA-4 antibodies deplete tumor Tregs selectively with partly better tolerability, which is moving them into Phase 2/3.

## Clinical data

Gotistobart has the strongest controlled data; every other next-gen readout is single-arm or early.

**Gotistobart (BioNTech / OncoC4): PRESERVE-003 stage 1, squamous NSCLC after PD-(L)1 (NCT05671510)**

- Design: 87 patients randomized 1:1 to gotistobart 6 mg/kg (after two 10 mg/kg loading doses) or docetaxel 75 mg/m², second line or later.
- WCLC 2026 update (Seoul, Sep 2026): median OS 18.5 vs 10.0 months; HR 0.572, a 43% lower risk of death. No new safety signals.
- Higher drug exposure tracked with longer survival, supporting the chosen dose.
- Earlier stage 1 analysis (Nature Medicine and ELCC 2026): about 54% lower risk of death, median OS not reached vs about 10 months, 12-month survival about 63% vs 30%, confirmed response rate 20% vs 4.8%.
- Pivotal stage: about 600 patients at about 160 sites. FDA Fast Track (2022) and Orphan Drug designation for squamous NSCLC (Jan 2026).
- Phase 1/2 PRESERVE-001 in PD-(L)1-resistant NSCLC: response rate about 29.6%, disease control 62.9%.

**Botensilimab + balstilimab (Agenus)**

- Neoadjuvant MSS colon cancer, NEST and UNICORN Phase 2s (per Agenus): pathologic response about 60–70%, major pathologic response about 35–40%, complete pathologic response about 30%.
- All treated patients were disease-free at 9–18 months median follow-up, with no disease or ctDNA recurrences in 38 patients. Grade ≥3 immune side effects were uncommon and surgery was rarely delayed.
- ROBBIN Phase 3: 850 patients randomized 1:1 to neoadjuvant BOT+BAL, then surgery and guideline-directed adjuvant chemo or observation, vs standard of care. Primary endpoint: event-free survival.
- About 1,300 patients have received BOT and/or BAL, with responses in nine metastatic late-line tumor types.
- Other 2026 readouts: 3-year survival in MSS metastatic CRC without active liver mets (ESMO GI, Jul 2026; 123-patient cohort), 3-year OS in recurrent ovarian cancer (IGCS, Oct 3, 2026), first Phase 2 melanoma data (ASCO 2026).

**Vilastobart + atezolizumab (Xilio): MSS metastatic CRC without liver mets**

- SITC 2025 (Nov 2025): 40% response rate (6 partial responses) in plasma TMB-high patients; tumors shrank by up to 71%. About 55% of MSS CRC is plasma TMB-high (≥10 mutations/Mb).
- ASCO 2025: 26% response rate in heavily pretreated patients.
- Safety across all treated patients: mostly grade 1–2; colitis 7%; 5% stopped for related side effects.

**Lorigerlimab (MacroGenics): LINNET, clear cell gynecologic cancers (May 7, 2026 data cut)**

- At 6 mg/kg every 3 weeks: 4 of 16 evaluable patients (25%) had confirmed partial responses; one later had an unconfirmed complete response.
- Grade ≥3 related side effects in 8 of 17 (47%); 2 (12%) stopped for side effects; no treatment-related deaths.
- Next: 20 more patients at 3 mg/kg, enrolled by end of 2026, with an update in 1H 2027. The Feb 23, 2026 partial hold was lifted Apr 8, 2026 with added blood and heart safety measures.

**GIGA-564 (GigaGen): first-in-human Phase 1 (AACR 2026)**

- 26 patients dosed and 22 evaluable at the Jan 28, 2026 data cut; favorable safety and early signs of tumor activity.

**Cadonilimab (Akeso)**

- Positive Phase 3s in first-line gastric cancer (Nature Medicine 2025) and first-line cervical cancer (AK104-303, progression-free survival).
- ELCC 2026: with anlotinib and docetaxel in PD-(L)1-resistant NSCLC, grade ≥3 related side effects in 14%.

**First-gen toxicity benchmarks**

- CheckMate-067 (melanoma): grade 3–4 related side effects in 59% with nivolumab + ipilimumab vs 21% with nivolumab alone.
- EORTC 18071 (adjuvant ipilimumab 10 mg/kg): about 1% treatment-related deaths.

**Why first-gen fell short**

- Toxicity capped doses and combinations; adjuvant high-dose ipilimumab (EORTC 18071) had about 1% treatment-related deaths.
- Cold tumors such as MSS colorectal cancer barely respond to checkpoint inhibitors.
- If anti-CTLA-4 works mainly by Fc-driven deletion of tumor Tregs, first-gen drugs took the systemic toxicity while missing much of the benefit. How well ipilimumab depletes Tregs in human tumors is still debated.

## Corrections and caveats

Two things said in the chat were corrected, and several figures need care before reuse.

- **MT-8421 timing (corrected):** The first answer called its IND clearance September 2026 news. The IND came before its 2025 Phase 1 data; the BioWorld page showed a retrieval date, not a publication date.
- **X handle sourcing (corrected):** The chat said both checked handles came from the companies' own sites. @bmsnews came from BMS's site, but @BioNTech\_Group came from OncoC4's site metadata.
- **Other BioWorld items:** The 2MW4691 bispecific and the gut-microbiota colitis study may be older than their page dates suggested. Treat their timing as unverified.
- **Gotistobart:** The survival data come from stage 1 (87 patients), not the \~600-patient pivotal stage. Two analyses circulate; the WCLC 2026 numbers (18.5 vs 10.0 months, HR 0.572) are the latest.
- **Agenus:** The neoadjuvant colon data are small and early, and >$7B is the company's own estimate. BATTMAN, a late-line MSS metastatic CRC Phase 3, began enrolling in spring 2026; Agenus stopped funding it in July. The graphics show BOT+BAL at Phase 2 with ROBBIN as a planned Phase 3.
- **Vilastobart:** The 40% response rate is a subset (plasma TMB-high, no liver mets, 15 patients evaluable for TMB). The 7% colitis and 5% discontinuation rates cover all treated patients.
- **Lorigerlimab:** The 47% grade ≥3 rate comes from the 17-patient clear cell cohort at 6 mg/kg.
- **Forecasts:** The $5.3B and $4.51B figures are rough third-party estimates. AstraZeneca's >$5B volrustomig projection predates the lung failure.
- **Yervoy revenue:** The 2011, 2017 and 2020 figures weren't re-checked against filings in the session.
- **General-knowledge facts:** The history entries, the CheckMate-067 and EORTC 18071 toxicity figures, the Medarex price and the 2024 FDA feedback to Agenus weren't sourced in the session.
- **Unchecked handles:** @AstraZeneca, @Merck, @myESMO and @IASLC are well known but weren't verified. Agenus, Xilio and MacroGenics link no X accounts on their sites, so the post uses their tickers; OncoC4 is private and named in text.

🕰️ 1. HOW WE GOT HERE

1987: CTLA-4 is discovered in Pierre Golstein's lab in Marseille. Nobody knows what it does yet.
1994–95: Jeffrey Bluestone's and James Allison's labs show it's a brake on T cells. Mice born without it die within weeks from runaway T-cell activation.
1996: Allison's lab blocks CTLA-4 in mice and their tumors are rejected. "Release the brakes" becomes a cancer strategy.
2009: $BMY buys Medarex, the developer of ipilimumab, for ~$2.4B.
2010: Ipilimumab becomes the first drug ever shown to extend survival in metastatic melanoma (Phase 3, NEJM).
2011: FDA approves Yervoy, the first immune checkpoint inhibitor. PD-1 drugs follow in 2014 and immunotherapy goes mainstream.
2018: Allison shares the Nobel Prize with Tasuku Honjo (PD-1).

Since then Yervoy has mostly worked as Opdivo's wingman: melanoma, kidney, lung, liver, MSI-H colorectal, mesothelioma and esophageal cancer. That combo business took it from $360M in its launch year to $2.9B in 2025.


💥 2. WHERE CTLA-4 FAILED

This is the part that matters, because it's exactly what next-gen is built to fix.

Toxicity. Blocking CTLA-4 unleashes T cells systemically: colitis, hepatitis, hypophysitis and more. In CheckMate-067 (melanoma), 59% of patients on nivo + ipi had grade 3–4 treatment-related events vs 21% on nivo alone. In the high-dose adjuvant ipi trial (EORTC 18071), about 1% of patients died from treatment-related side effects. That forced low doses and limited what CTLA-4 could be combined with.

Phase 3 flops. A partial list:
• Tremelimumab (then at Pfizer) in melanoma, 2008
• Ipilimumab in prostate cancer (twice), small cell lung cancer, and squamous lung cancer with chemo, 2014–2017
• Tremelimumab ± durvalumab in mesothelioma (DETERMINE), lung (MYSTIC, NEPTUNE) and head & neck (EAGLE), 2016–2019
• Nivo + ipi in SCLC maintenance (CheckMate-451), adjuvant melanoma (CheckMate-915), head & neck (CheckMate-651) and adjuvant kidney cancer (CheckMate-914), 2018–2022

Cold tumors. About 95% of metastatic colorectal cancers are microsatellite stable (MSS), and checkpoint inhibitors have done almost nothing for them.

The biology twist. Around 2013, mouse studies showed anti-CTLA-4 works largely by deleting Tregs inside tumors, via Fc receptors on NK cells and macrophages, not just by blocking a signal. How well ipilimumab does that in human tumors is still debated. The theory: first-gen drugs took all of the systemic toxicity while leaving a lot of the tumor-killing potential on the table.


🛠️ 3. WHAT CHANGED: THE NEXT-GEN PLAYBOOK

🎯 Fc-enhanced antibodies that recruit immune killer cells to wipe out Tregs inside the tumor (botensilimab, porustobart, vilastobart)
🔁 pH-sensitive binding that lets go of CTLA-4 inside the cell so it recycles to the surface instead of being destroyed. Tregs in healthy tissue keep working while Tregs in the tumor get deleted (gotistobart)
🎭 Masked, tumor-activated antibodies that only switch on where tumor proteases cut the mask off (vilastobart)
⏱️ Short half-life (~4–5 days for porustobart) so any side effects don't linger
🧩 PD-1 + CTLA-4 (and even VEGF) in a single molecule (cadonilimab, volrustomig, lorigerlimab, CS2009)
🚫 Non-blocking Treg depleters that skip ligand blockade entirely (GIGA-564)

A 2026 Annual Review of Medicine paper put it simply: Fc-optimized anti-CTLA-4s selectively deplete tumor Tregs with partly improved tolerability, which is why they're now moving through Phase 2/3.


🧪 4. THE ASSETS: WHO'S WHERE

ON THE MARKET
• Yervoy (ipilimumab), $BMY: approved 2011, used with Opdivo across 7 tumor types
• Imjudo (tremelimumab), $AZN: approved 2022 with Imfinzi in liver and lung cancer
• Cadonilimab, Akeso (9926.HK): PD-1 × CTLA-4 bispecific, approved in China since 2022 (cervical, gastric). A ~900-patient global first-line gastric Phase 3 vs chemo ± nivolumab is starting

PHASE 3
• Gotistobart (ONC-392 / BNT316), $BNTX + OncoC4: pivotal PRESERVE-003 in squamous NSCLC after PD-(L)1 (~600 patients), plus a Phase 2 in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer. FDA Fast Track and Orphan Drug designations. BioNTech paid $200M upfront to partner in 2023
• Volrustomig, $AZN: PD-1 × CTLA-4 bispecific with Phase 3s in cervical, head & neck and mesothelioma (that one uses nivo + ipi as a comparator). The first-line lung Phase 3 was stopped in August

• Botensilimab + balstilimab, $AGEN: Fc-enhanced, ~1,300 patients treated with BOT and/or BAL so far. Registrational Phase 3 ROBBIN in neoadjuvant MSS colon cancer (850 patients) is set to dose its first patient in Q1 2027

PHASE 2

• Lorigerlimab, $MGNX: PD-1 × CTLA-4 DART in gynecologic cancers
• CS2009, CStone (2616.HK): PD-1 × VEGF × CTLA-4 trispecific in lung and colorectal cancer (Phase 1/2)

PHASE 1

BIOSIMILAR
• HLX13 (ipilimumab biosimilar), Henlius / Sandoz ($SDZNY): Phase 1/3 in liver cancer


📈 5. THE DATA THAT HAS PEOPLE EXCITED

🫁 Gotistobart in squamous NSCLC after PD-(L)1 (PRESERVE-003 stage 1, 87 patients, WCLC 2026)
• Median OS 18.5 vs 10.0 months vs docetaxel
• HR 0.572, a 43% lower risk of death
• Response rate 20% vs 4.8% (earlier Nature Medicine analysis)
• Higher drug exposure tracked with longer survival
• Chemo-free, single agent

🩺 Botensilimab + balstilimab before surgery in MSS colon cancer (NEST + UNICORN, per Agenus)
• ~30% pathologic complete response
• ~35–40% major pathologic response
• Every treated patient disease-free at 9–18 months median follow-up
In a tumor where checkpoint inhibitors have been close to inert, that's a real signal.

🎭 Vilastobart + atezolizumab in MSS colorectal cancer without liver mets and with high plasma TMB (SITC 2025)
• 40% response rate (6 partial responses)
• Colitis in 7%, discontinuation for side effects in 5%

🎗️ Lorigerlimab in clear cell gynecologic cancers (LINNET, May 2026 data cut)
• 25% response rate (4 of 16)


⚠️ 6. THE REALITY CHECK

Next-gen isn't a sure thing, and pretending otherwise is how people get burned.
• $AZN stopped volrustomig's first-line lung Phase 3 in August after an interim look said it was unlikely to beat Keytruda + chemo. AZ had projected >$5B in peak sales, and Leerink had questioned whether the drug has a workable therapeutic window
• $MGNX's lorigerlimab hit a partial clinical hold in February (lifted in April) and had grade ≥3 treatment-related events in 47% of patients. It's now testing a lower dose
• $AGEN has been here before. The FDA discouraged an accelerated filing in late-line MSS colorectal cancer in 2024, and in July Agenus pulled funding from its late-line BATTMAN Phase 3 to go all-in on the neoadjuvant setting
• Gotistobart's survival data come from stage 1 of the trial (87 patients). The ~600-patient pivotal stage is what will count
• The neoadjuvant colon data are early and small. ROBBIN's interim event-free survival analysis isn't expected until 2H 2029
