### Post 2 · FDA decision Nov 14 · 1,237 characters

```text
2/ Nov 14: the FDA decision

The filing: ivonescimab + chemo for EGFR-mutant non-squamous NSCLC that progressed after a 3rd-gen EGFR TKI like Tagrisso, based on Summit's global Phase 3 HARMONi. (Nov 14 is a Saturday, so news could come earlier.)

The data:
• PFS: HR 0.52, median 6.8 vs 4.4 months
• OS at the primary analysis: HR 0.79, p=0.057, short of statistical significance
• Updated analysis announced in July 2026 (extra follow-up for Western patients only; no p-value given): OS HR 0.76 overall, and 0.76 in both Western and Asian patients

The catch: back in May 2025, Summit disclosed that the FDA said a statistically significant OS benefit is necessary to support approval. Summit filed anyway, arguing the overall benefit-risk is favorable, and the FDA accepted the BLA in January.

Why it matters:
• Approval = Summit's first product revenue and the first PD-1×VEGF bispecific on the US market
• It's a later-line setting where J&J's Rybrevant + chemo is already approved (AZ/Daiichi's Datroway is approved one line later, after chemo), so near-term sales should be modest
• A rejection would most likely hinge on OS. It would delay revenue, but it doesn't decide the first-line program, where most of the value sits
```

### Post 3 · HARMONi-3 vs Keytruda · 1,321 characters

```text
3/ The big one: HARMONi-3

Ivonescimab + chemo vs Keytruda + chemo in first-line metastatic NSCLC, run globally, with two separately powered cohorts:
• Squamous (~600 pts): events for the final PFS analysis expected in H2 2026, with an early OS look. Guggenheim has flagged that the public topline could slip to early 2027.
• Non-squamous (~1,000 pts): PFS events expected in H1 2027.

Why it matters: first-line NSCLC is the heart of the Keytruda franchise ($31.7B in 2025 sales, all uses). A win turns ivonescimab from a niche launch into a real challenger to the PD-1 standard.

The read-through from China is strong, but not a guarantee:
• HARMONi-6 (Akeso; squamous; vs a PD-1 + chemo): OS HR 0.66, p=0.0017. Summit calls it the first Phase 3 to beat a PD-(L)1 + chemo regimen on survival.
• China doesn't always translate: HARMONi-A hit OS in China (HR 0.74); the global HARMONi didn't (HR 0.79).
• A Q2 interim PFS analysis of the squamous cohort didn't clear its deliberately high bar for an early win; the monitoring committee said to continue to the final analysis.

Also enrolling: HARMONi-7, ivonescimab alone vs Keytruda alone in PD-L1-high first-line NSCLC (~780 pts; timing not guided). In China, HARMONi-2 showed median OS of 30.8 vs 22.6 months against Keytruda (HR 0.73) in PD-L1-positive patients.
```

### Post 5 · AstraZeneca and cash · 1,155 characters

```text
5/ The AstraZeneca deal changed the funding picture

On Sept 28, AZ agreed to invest $2.0B in convertible preferred stock, equal to ~12% of Summit's common shares (10.6% fully diluted), at $18.36/share: an 18.6% premium to that day's $15.48 close. Conversion awaits antitrust (HSR) clearance where needed and a shareholder vote to authorize more shares.

What AZ gets: equity, preemptive rights, combo trials (starting with sone-ve in GI cancers) and a non-binding MOU for more ADC combos.
What it doesn't get: rights to ivonescimab. Each company keeps its own drugs.

Why it matters: in its Q2 10-Q, Summit flagged that its cash wasn't enough to fund a full year of planned operations. Rough pro forma (my math, assuming the deal closes): $690.7M in cash and investments at June 30 + $68.4M in gross ATM proceeds after quarter-end + $2.0B ≈ $2.76B. That's ~4.5 years of Q2-level non-GAAP opex ($151.8M a quarter), before Q3 spending, rising trial and launch costs, and any milestones owed to Akeso.

One more detail: per the 8-K, AZ also gets certain rights if a third party makes an acquisition proposal for Summit. No such proposal has been announced.
```

### Post 6 · Markets today · 1,033 characters

```text
6/ Where the markets are today

Lung cancer (Summit's core):
• US 2026: ~229K new lung cancer cases and ~125K deaths expected (ACS), ~77% NSCLC. It's the leading cause of US cancer death, about 1 in 5.
• NSCLC drug sales across the 7 major markets (US, EU4, UK, Japan): ~$33B in 2025 (DelveInsight)
• Keytruda + chemo is the first-line standard; Tagrisso, the EGFR-mutant leader, sold ~$7.3B in 2025
• Adenocarcinoma is ~45% of US lung cancers and squamous ~21% (CDC, 2022 data)

Colorectal: ~1.9M new cases a year worldwide and the #2 cancer killer (~900K deaths, IARC 2022). For most patients the first-line standard is still Avastin or cetuximab + chemo; checkpoint drugs mainly help the small MSI-high subset.

Biliary: far smaller, but Imfinzi + chemo is the standard ivonescimab just beat in China.

Territory matters: Summit has the Americas, Europe, the Middle East, Africa and Japan. Akeso keeps China and the remaining markets, and says 100,000+ patients have already received ivonescimab, mostly commercially in China.
```

### Post 7 · Competition · 1,098 characters · attach the competitive landscape

```text
7/ The competition: everyone wants a PD-(L)1×VEGF now

Since ivonescimab beat Keytruda in China, big pharma has paid up for look-alikes:
• $BMY: $1.5B upfront + $2B guaranteed to BioNTech for pumitamig (up to $11.1B). Pivotal trials in ~8 tumor types
• $PFE: $1.25B upfront to 3SBio for PF-08634404. Three Phase 3s started in 2025, more planned
• $ABBV: $650M upfront to RemeGen for RC148 (Jan 2026), still early clinical
• $MRK: $588M upfront to LaNova for MK-2010 (LM-299); first data at AACR in April, no Phase 3 announced then

Summit's edge is time: the class's first FDA filing, five ivonescimab Phase 3 readouts that each hit a primary endpoint (four run by Akeso in China), and a fully enrolled HARMONi-3, while rivals' first-line lung Phase 3s started more recently.

The bigger incumbent is still Keytruda ($31.7B in 2025; US exclusivity expected to end ~2028), plus ADCs pushing into first-line lung: AZ/Daiichi's Datroway, Merck's sac-TMT, Pfizer's sigvotatug vedotin.

In EGFR-mutant disease after TKIs, a launch would face Rybrevant + chemo, with Datroway approved one line later.
```

### Post 8 · Growth outlook · 906 characters · attach the projections

```text
8/ Where the market is heading

Patients: worldwide new lung cancer cases are projected to rise from ~2.48M (2022) to ~4.62M by 2050 if today's rates hold, with deaths nearly doubling to ~3.55M (GLOBOCAN-based). It's mostly an aging and population-growth story, and much of it is in Asia: China alone is projected at ~1.8M cases a year by 2050, which is Akeso's territory. In the US, case counts have been falling as smoking declines (ACS), though an aging population could push them back up.

Dollars: NSCLC drug sales in the 7 major markets are projected to roughly double, from ~$33B (2025) to ~$67B by 2036, ~6.6% a year (DelveInsight), driven by new combos, ADCs and bispecifics.

What that means for $SMMT: in its territories, upside comes less from more patients and more from share, replacing PD-1 backbones as Keytruda faces biosimilars. That's why HARMONi-3 and HARMONi-7 matter more than Nov 14.
```

### Post 9 · Risks + bottom line · 773 characters

```text
9/ What could go wrong
• OS: HARMONi missed its OS endpoint, and the FDA said significant OS was needed. Don't assume approval on Nov 14.
• Translation: China results have looked better than global ones so far (HARMONi-A vs HARMONi).
• Timing: HARMONi-3 squamous is guided for H2 2026, but the topline could slip into 2027.
• Competition: BMS/BioNTech, Pfizer, AbbVie and Merck have far deeper pockets.
• Concentration: one licensed asset, with milestones and royalties owed to Akeso.
• Dilution and control: AZ's $2B is new stock on top of ATM sales, and Summit is a "controlled company" whose co-CEO is its principal stockholder.

Bottom line: Nov 14 is the first test, but HARMONi-3 is the readout that sets the value.

Not investment advice. Do your own research.
```

    if "Inter" in f and "Display" not in f:

OUT = "."                            # output folder
W, H, DPI = 1080, 1350, 200          # 10.8 x 13.5 in at 200 dpi -> 2160 x 2700 px

_texts = []


def canvas():
    fig = plt.figure(figsize=(W / 100, H / 100), dpi=DPI)
    fig.patch.set_facecolor(SURF)
    ax = fig.add_axes([0, 0, 1, 1])
    ax.set_xlim(0, W)
    ax.set_ylim(H, 0)

def T(ax, x, y, s, size, color=INK, weight=400, ha="left", va="baseline", **kw):
    t = ax.text(x, y, s, fontsize=size * 0.72, color=color, fontweight=weight,
                ha=ha, va=va, **kw)
    _texts.append(t)
    return t


def width(ax, s, size, weight=400):
    t = ax.text(0, 0, s, fontsize=size * 0.72, fontweight=weight)
    bb = t.get_window_extent(renderer=ax.figure.canvas.get_renderer())
    t.remove()
    return bb.width / (DPI / 100)

                                linewidth=0, facecolor=color, zorder=z))


def diamond(ax, x, yc, r, color, z=4):
    ax.add_patch(Polygon([(x, yc - r), (x + r, yc), (x, yc + r), (x - r, yc)],
                         closed=True, facecolor=color, edgecolor=SURF, linewidth=2.2, zorder=z))


def check_and_save(fig, ax, name):

The X character counts came from this function, the same logic as the original count.py:

```python
import re
import unicodedata

URL = re.compile(r"https?://\S+")
RANGES = [(0, 4351), (8192, 8205), (8208, 8223), (8242, 8247)]


def x_count(text: str) -> int:
    """Characters as X counts them: most Latin text 1, emoji and other symbols 2, each link 23."""
    text = unicodedata.normalize("NFC", text)
    urls = URL.findall(text)
    body = URL.sub("", text)
    weight = sum(1 if any(lo <= ord(c) <= hi for lo, hi in RANGES) else 2 for c in body)
    return weight + 23 * len(urls)
```
