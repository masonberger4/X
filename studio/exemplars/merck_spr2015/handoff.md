## The deal

On Sept 28, 2026, Merck licensed global rights to SPR2015, a preclinical oral KRAS G12D inhibitor, from SciBrunch Therapeutics for $400M upfront and up to $2.13B in total.

| Term | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Licensor | [SciBrunch Therapeutics](https://allsci.com/news/biotech/kras-g12d-inhibitor-merck-acquires-preclinical/), Shanghai; founded late 2024 by Tao Hu (CEO) and Yang Zhang (CSO); about $65M raised in two rounds before the deal |
| Licensee | Merck & Co. (MSD outside the US and Canada) |
| Rights | Exclusive worldwide rights to develop, manufacture and commercialize |
| Upfront | $400M: about 19% of headline value and about 6x SciBrunch's prior funding |
| Total value | Up to $2.13B, with development, commercial and other milestones across multiple indications |
| Accounting | $400M pre-tax charge, about $0.13 a share, in Merck's Q3 2026 GAAP and non-GAAP results |
| Status | Closed at announcement; BofA Securities advised SciBrunch |

Terms are from [Merck's release](https://www.merck.com/?p=3699961) and [AllSci](https://allsci.com/news/biotech/kras-g12d-inhibitor-merck-acquires-preclinical/).

SPR2015 at a glance:

- **Mechanism:** an oral molecular glue that binds KRAS G12D in its active (ON) state by forming a complex with cyclophilin A, the same tri-complex approach Revolution Medicines uses.
- **Data so far:** preclinical only. Nanomolar activity in G12D-mutant cell lines, selectivity over normal KRAS, and single-agent antitumor activity in cell-line and patient-derived xenografts.
- **Target cancers:** pancreatic, colorectal and non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC).
- **Next step:** first-in-human dosing, likely in 2027 (author's estimate).
- **SciBrunch's other lead asset:** SPR1020, a brain-penetrant PARP1 inhibitor in Phase 1/2.

One company profile ([ChemXplore](https://chemxplore.com/companies/scibrunch-therapeutics)) lists the deal at $2.53B; Merck's release says $2.13B, which the post uses.

The other big deal on Oct 2 was not oncology: [Novartis licensed an early-stage mRNA autoimmune therapy from China's Abogen](https://cnbc.com/2026/10/02/novartis-abogen-china-biotech-deal.html) for $575M upfront and up to $7.8B in total. No outright solid tumor acquisitions turned up that day.

## Why Merck did it

The post's read: after balking at about $30B for Revolution Medicines, Merck bought a $400M option on the same mechanism.

1. **The front door closed.** On Jan 8, 2026, the [FT reported](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/merck-reportedly-in-talks-to-buy-revolution-in-up-to-32-billion-deal-ce7e59d2d981ff24) Merck in talks to buy Revolution for $28–32B. Talks [reportedly ended over price](https://www.biocentury.com/article/658179/revolution-s-shares-dip-with-report-of-merck-walking-away-deals-report) around Jan 26, and Revolution's shares fell 17%. The SPR2015 upfront is about 1.3% of $30B.
2. **Keytruda's clock.** Keytruda [starts losing patent protection in 2028](https://www.thepharmaletter.com/biotech-news/brief-merck-and-co-reportedly-planning-buy-of-revolution-medicines), so Merck needs oncology assets with long runways. A drug entering the clinic in 2027 likely has patent life into the 2040s (author's inference).
3. **A RAS franchise built around Keytruda.** [Calderasib](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260107533765/en) (MK-1084, a G12C inhibitor, partnered with Taiho and Astex) is in four Phase 3s: KANDLELIT-004 (NSCLC with high PD-L1, with Keytruda), -007 (first-line NSCLC with Keytruda Qlex vs chemo-immunotherapy, about 675 patients), -012 (first-line colorectal with cetuximab and mFOLFOX6) and [-015](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07554339) (unresected locally advanced NSCLC after chemoradiation, with durvalumab). G12C is the most common KRAS mutation in lung cancer; SPR2015 adds G12D, the most common overall.
4. **Cheap shots on goal from China.** Chinese biotechs supply early molecules quickly and at lower prices; the same week, Novartis paid $575M upfront to Abogen.
5. **The structure signals urgency.** About 19% of value upfront, no option and an immediate close suggest Merck wanted the asset locked up before others could bid.

AbbVie was also reported as a Revolution suitor in January and denied it. Merck's other recent deals include Verona Pharma (about $10B) and Cidara ($9.2B), both in 2025.

## Market share outlook

If SPR2015 succeeds, the base case is 10–15% of the G12D-targeted market at peak, about $0.4–0.6B a year; taking the lead is a long shot.

| Scenario | What it takes | Peak share | Illustrative sales a year |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Me-too | Works, but no clear edge and launches 6th or later | About 5% | About $0.2B |
| Base case, most likely | Good data plus Merck's global reach and Keytruda combinations | 10–15% | $0.4–0.6B |
| Bull case | Clear tolerability or potency edge; wins colorectal and lung combinations | 20–25% | $0.8–1.0B |
| Takes the lead, long shot | Displaces Revolution as the G12D leader | 30%+ | $1.2B+ |

Sales = share × Data Bridge's $3.97B forecast for 2033, for scale only; peak sales would come later, in a market that could be bigger or smaller. Scenarios are the author's opinion, not investment advice.

Why the ceiling is limited:

- **Timing:** approval likely in the early-to-mid 2030s, after daraxonrasib and probably zoldonrasib are entrenched.
- **Same mechanism:** a similar drug that arrives later rarely takes the lead.
- **Pan-RAS erosion:** if daraxonrasib becomes standard across RAS-mutant pancreatic cancer, G12D-selective drugs shift to combinations or later lines.
- **Competitor messaging:** Verastem already pitches ON/OFF inhibition as better than ON-only drugs like SPR2015.

Where it could win:

- **Tolerability:** rash, mouth sores and GI toxicity are the known costs of RAS(ON) drugs; a cleaner drug matters most for long use, such as adjuvant or maintenance therapy.
- **Oral dosing** against Astellas' IV degrader.
- **Combinations** with Keytruda or Merck's ADCs, especially in colorectal cancer, where Revolution's pivotal trials are not focused.
- **Commercial reach** outside the US, especially if Revolution stays independent.
- **Resistance:** a second-generation drug that works after first-line RAS(ON) drugs fail could own later lines.

Signposts to watch:

1. IND filing and first-in-human timing.
2. Phase 1 response rate and tolerability in pretreated G12D pancreatic cancer, against zoldonrasib's 30%.
3. Whether Merck starts Keytruda or ADC combination studies early.
4. Phase 3 readouts for zoldonrasib, INCB161734 and setidegrasib; positive results start closing the first-line window.
5. Revolution's future: if Merck re-engages, SPR2015 becomes a backup; if a rival buys Revolution, SPR2015 becomes Merck's main RAS(ON) bet.

## Final X post

This is the post as of Oct 4, with cashtags, verified handles, hashtags and two accuracy fixes (see Corrections). It is plain text, so it pastes cleanly into X.

```text
Merck $MRK just paid $400 million in cash for a cancer drug that has never been tested in a single human being.

Eight months ago, the same company reportedly walked away from a roughly $30 billion deal for the leader in this exact field.

Put those two facts side by side and you understand most of the SPR2015 deal. Here's my read.

THE DEAL

On Sept 28, @Merck licensed global rights to SPR2015 from SciBrunch Therapeutics, a Shanghai biotech founded in late 2024. $400M upfront, up to $2.13B total with milestones. It's already closed, and Merck is booking a $400M pre-tax charge in Q3, about 13 cents a share.

A few things jumped out at me:

• SciBrunch had raised about $65M before this. The upfront alone is more than 6x everything they'd raised as a company.
• About 19% of the headline value is paid on day one. For a preclinical asset, that's rich. Early deals are usually milestone-heavy precisely because nobody knows yet if the molecule works in people.
• No option, no staged structure. Merck paid and closed. My read: it wanted this locked up before anyone else could bid.

WHAT SPR2015 ACTUALLY IS

SPR2015 is an oral molecular glue that hits KRAS G12D in its active "ON" state. It sticks the mutant KRAS protein to a helper protein inside the cell, cyclophilin A, so the complex can't signal. If that sounds familiar, it should. It's the same tri-complex playbook Revolution Medicines $RVMD built its whole company on.

The preclinical package is solid: nanomolar potency in G12D cancer cell lines, selectivity over normal KRAS, and single-agent anti-tumor activity in both cell-line and patient-derived models.

But anyone who's been around drug development long enough has watched beautiful mouse data die in Phase 1. G12D is a hard target. Bristol Myers $BMY dropped MRTX1133, the G12D inhibitor it got with Mirati, because its pharmacokinetics in patients were too variable. Good xenografts are the entry ticket, not the prize.

WHY G12D IS THE PRIZE

G12D is the most common KRAS mutation in human cancer, somewhere between 26% and 36% of all KRAS mutations depending on whose numbers you use. It's the most common KRAS variant in pancreatic and colorectal cancer and shows up in lung cancer too.

One forecast puts the G12D-targeted market at about $842M in 2025, growing to roughly $4B by 2033, a 21% annual clip (chart 1).

I'd take those numbers with a big grain of salt. No G12D-selective drug is approved in the U.S. yet, so that 2025 number is a model, not sales. Another firm puts the ENTIRE KRAS-inhibitor market at $602M for 2025. The G12D slice can't be bigger than the whole pie. The forecasts don't agree with each other.

Honestly, I think they're too small, not too big. On Aug 26, the FDA approved Revolution's daraxonrasib (Rasonque) for previously treated metastatic pancreatic cancer, 6.5 months ahead of its deadline. In Phase 3, median survival was 13.2 months vs 6.7 on chemo. Nearly double, in a cancer where almost nothing has moved the needle in decades. A drug class that does that doesn't stay in second line. The real money gets decided in first-line pancreatic, colorectal and lung, and eventually in earlier-stage disease.

THE PROBLEM: MERCK IS STARTING FROM THE BACK

The field as of today (chart 2):

• Revolution Medicines $RVMD: daraxonrasib (pan-RAS) is approved. Zoldonrasib, its G12D-selective drug and SPR2015's closest relative, is in Phase 3 in first-line pancreatic cancer, with more pivotal trials in lung and as a doublet with daraxonrasib this year. It posted a 30% response rate on its own in previously treated pancreatic cancer, and 63% with FOLFIRINOX in a small first-line cohort.
• Incyte $INCY (@Incyte): INCB161734 started a first-line pancreatic Phase 3 in Q1.
• Astellas: setidegrasib, a G12D degrader, now has two Phase 3s running (first-line pancreatic, second-line lung). It's an IV infusion, which matters later.
• Verastem $VSTM (@VerastemOncolog) and GenFleet: VS-7375, an oral ON/OFF G12D inhibitor, has three registration-directed Phase 2s in pancreatic, lung and colorectal, Fast Track in two of them, and a Phase 3 in China.
• Hengrui's HRS-4642 is also in Phase 3 in China.
• Behind them: Lilly, AstraZeneca, Roche, Bayer/Kumquat, Arvinas and plenty of others in early clinical work.

SPR2015 is preclinical. First-in-human is realistically a 2027 event. My best guess for a first approval is 2031 to 2033. By then I'd expect several G12D drugs on the market, plus a pan-RAS drug that's already standard of care.

One detail that didn't get enough attention: Verastem is already pitching its drug as superior to "ON-only" G12D inhibitors in preclinical head-to-heads. SPR2015 is an ON-state inhibitor. The competitive marketing is already written.

SO WHY DID MERCK DO IT?

This is the part I find most interesting.

1. It already tried the expensive door. In January, Merck was in talks to buy Revolution for $28–32B. By late January, those talks had reportedly died over price. $400M upfront is about 1.3% of that. If you can't buy the leader, you buy a cheap option on the same mechanism.

2. Keytruda. It starts losing U.S. patent protection in 2028, and Merck needs oncology assets with long runways. A molecule entering the clinic in 2027 likely has patent life well into the 2040s.

3. It's building a RAS franchise around Keytruda. Merck already has calderasib, a G12C inhibitor, in four Phase 3 trials, including combos with Keytruda in lung and with cetuximab plus chemo in colorectal. Add a G12D drug and Merck covers the most common KRAS mutation overall plus the most common one in lung cancer, paired with the biggest immunotherapy franchise in oncology.

4. China is where the cheap shots on goal are. The same week, Novartis $NVS paid $575M upfront for an mRNA program from China's Abogen. Big Pharma keeps sourcing early molecules from Chinese biotechs because the speed and price are hard to match anywhere else.

IF IT WORKS, HOW MUCH CAN MERCK TAKE?

My scenarios, assuming SPR2015 actually delivers in trials (chart 3):

• Me-too profile, launches 6th or later: around 5% of the G12D-targeted market
• Base case, good data plus Merck's commercial machine and Keytruda combos: 10–15%
• Bull case, clearly better tolerability or potency, wins combos in colorectal and lung: 20–25%
• Takes the lead from Revolution: 30%+. I'd call that a long shot.

For scale: applied to the roughly $4B 2033 forecast, the base case is $400–600M a year and the bull case is $800M–1B. That's a good product. It's not a Keytruda replacement.

Where could it actually win? Oral dosing vs an IV degrader. Tolerability, since rash, mouth sores and GI side effects are the known tax on RAS(ON) drugs. And colorectal, where G12D is the most common KRAS variant and Revolution's pivotal program is focused elsewhere.

WHAT I'M WATCHING

1. IND and first-in-human timing. Every quarter matters in this race.
2. Phase 1 response rates in pretreated G12D pancreatic cancer. The bar is zoldonrasib's 30%.
3. Whether Merck goes straight to Keytruda or ADC combos instead of a slow monotherapy path.
4. Phase 3 readouts for zoldonrasib, INCB161734 and setidegrasib over the next few years. If they hit, the first-line window starts closing.
5. Whether Revolution stays independent. If Merck circles back, SPR2015 becomes a backup. If someone else buys Revolution, SPR2015 becomes Merck's main RAS(ON) bet.

BOTTOM LINE

Merck didn't buy a pancreatic cancer drug. It bought a cheap ticket into the RAS(ON) era after balking at the price of the whole stadium. For a company Merck's size, $400M is a manageable bet with real optionality. Whether that ticket ends up courtside or in the nosebleeds gets decided in Phase 1, not in a press release.
```
